# High-Performance-Based Perovskite-Supported Nanocomposite for the Development of Green Energy Device Applications: An Overview

## problem
- Combating global energy demand and warming requires cost-effective, environmentally friendly, high-performance energy-storage and energy-conversion technologies, but research on perovskite-based electrode materials for this purpose is scattered across many separate studies on different device types.
- The review aims to consolidate the prospects, synthesis strategies, structural properties, and electrochemical performance of perovskite-supported nanocomposite materials across the major green-energy device categories: supercapacitors, fuel cells, solid oxide fuel cells (SOFCs), batteries, and solar cells.

## method
- This is a literature review, not a primary experimental study: the authors survey and synthesize findings from many previously published studies on perovskite-supported nanocomposite electrode materials.
- The review is organized by application area — electrode surface studies, supercapacitors, fuel cells, SOFCs, batteries, and solar cells — summarizing synthesis strategies (e.g., sol-gel, electrospinning, mechano-chemical reactions), structural/morphological characterization methods (e.g., HRTEM, XPS, SEM), and electrochemical evaluation techniques (e.g., cyclic voltammetry, galvanostatic charge–discharge) used across the cited studies.

## dataset
- The review does not report original experimental data of its own (its Data Availability Statement states "Not applicable"); it instead compiles specific capacitance, energy/power density, power conversion efficiency (PCE), and cyclic-stability figures reported by numerous individually cited primary studies.

## results
- Perovskite-based solar cells' power conversion efficiency (PCE) values are reported to have increased from 2.2% to 22.1% across the cited literature.
- A CdS quantum-dot-based organometallic halide perovskite supercapacitor electrode achieved an areal capacitance of 141 µF/cm², energy density of 23.8 nWh/cm², and power density of 12.7 mW/cm², with stability over 4000 charge–discharge cycles.
- A PrBaMn1.7Co0.3O5+δ/Co3O4 composite (Co3O4-PBMCO) electrode demonstrated an outstanding specific capacity of 1571 F/g with good cyclic stability over 20,000 cycles.
- A CeMnO3 nanofiber anode for lithium-ion batteries achieved a discharge capacity of 2159 mAh/g with 93.79% coulombic efficiency, retaining a reversible capacity of 395 mAh/g at 200 mA/g after 60 cycles at a 1000 mA/g discharge rate.

## limitations
- Fuel cells generally face drawbacks such as high cost, environmental pollution, lower anodic catalytic activity, and poor cyclic stability.
- Perovskite materials synthesized by the traditional sol-gel method tend to have small surface areas, which limits catalytic site utilization and reduces device performance (e.g., in Li-O2 batteries).
- There is a tradeoff between particle size and stability: smaller particles improve catalytic efficiency and surface area, but are less thermally/chemically stable and can be destroyed by thermal or chemical treatments.
- As a review article, the paper synthesizes and reports on findings from other primary studies rather than presenting new original synthesis or performance data of its own.
